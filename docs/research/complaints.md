# Voice of the Customer: IEEPA Tariff Refund Complaints from US Small Importers, E-commerce Sellers and Courier Customers (2026)

Research date: Oct 25, 2026. Scope: complaints, pain points and how well existing fixes work. CBP rule mechanics and vendor teardowns are covered by other researchers and are left out here, except where a rule is the cause of a complaint.

**Method and limitations (read first):**
- **Reddit could not be read directly.** Fetching reddit.com and old.reddit.com was blocked by the session allowlist. Several `site:reddit.com` and "reddit ..." searches returned **zero Reddit URLs**; the engine returned news and law-firm pages instead. So **no Reddit thread counts, upvotes or comment counts are available**. The same is true of Hacker News (no HN results), X/Twitter and LinkedIn (not reachable), and Amazon Seller Central forums (not reached).
- **Forum evidence is thin.** It comes from eBay Community, Shopify Community and one hobbyist forum (MavicPilots). EcommerceBytes, the main eBay/Etsy seller news site, has shut down: its Apr 20, 2026 article on carrier refunds now shows only a closure notice.
- **Most of the voice-of-customer evidence comes from secondary sources:** named business owners quoted in news (AP, Reuters, NPR, Bloomberg Law, Business Insider, CBS, FT, Fortune, local outlets), trade-group statements, congressional letters, and CBP data filed in court.
- **The session's web-search budget ran out** before the last planned queries (awareness surveys, Seller Central threads). Volume estimates below are therefore **counts of distinct sources and named complainants I found**, not measures of social-media volume.
- **Labels used:** "Evidence" = cited. "Inference" = my reasoning, marked as such.

## Q1. Where are people complaining, and what exactly do they say?

### Takeaway
Reachable complaints are concentrated in news stories quoting named small-business owners (about 30 named businesses and consumers found), in congressional letters (at least 5), in trade-group statements (Main Street Alliance, We Pay the Tariffs, AAEI, NFIB) and in CBP's own court-filed statistics. Open-forum complaints (Reddit, HN, Seller Central) could not be measured. Complaints run in three waves: Feb–Mar ("will I ever see this money / I can't afford lawyers"), Apr–May ("the portal crashed / I can't log in / my claim was rejected") and Jun–Oct ("I'm still waiting / I'm not the importer of record / my entries are finally liquidated and shut out").

### Cited Findings

Wild Rye (outerwear): "The time suck on our team has been monumental." Princess Awesome paid about $30K and raised $8K through a tip jar. — [Reuters via AJOT](https://www.ajot.com/news/for-some-small-businesses-a-tariff-refund-isnt-worth-the-pain-of-pursuing-it)

**Trade groups and surveys:**

- Atlanta Fed Survey of Business Uncertainty, Aug 10–21, 2026 (all firm sizes, not small-business specific): about 25% of firms had applied for or received refunds. Of 220 that described refund plans, 17% are giving customers rebates and about 15% are lowering prices. — [CPA Practice Advisor](https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/09/22/most-businesses-receiving-tariff-refunds-are-holding-onto-the-cash/190394/)
- AAEI (Eugene Laney): small and medium businesses "don't have a trade compliance department". — [TPM](https://talkingpointsmemo.com/?p=1542887)

**Forums (weak signal):**

- The Shopify Community thread I found (Mar 2025) concerns an ordinary customs-duty refund on a cancelled order, not IEEPA. — [Shopify Community](https://community.shopify.com/t/customs-duty-and-refund/401928)

### Inferences
- (Inference) Search engines index few or no Reddit/HN/Seller Central threads on this topic. That points to a search-engine indexing limit, not an absence of complaints; volume there remains unknown. Distress signals are strong in news. The courier-refund "where's my money" complaints (consumers, Etsy/eBay buyers) likely live mostly on Reddit and X, which I could not measure.
- (Inference) The tone shifted over the year. Access and portal complaints peaked in April–May and faded once CBP added a self-service password reset and callbacks. Complaints about waiting, exclusion and "not my entry" grew from June to October.

### Gaps
- No Reddit, HN, X, LinkedIn or Amazon Seller Central thread counts, upvotes or quotes (blocked or not indexed).
- No survey measuring *awareness* of refund eligibility among small importers. The search budget ran out before I could find one.
- No primary text of the Sep 9 letter.

## Q2. Complaint categories (taxonomy) with rough volume and solution status

### Takeaway
I found 16 complaint categories. By evidence volume (distinct sources), the biggest are: courier pass-through waits and confusion, ACE/portal access, IOR mismatch for indirect importers, entries locked out by liquidation or deadlines, ACH-stranded refunds, CAPE validation rejections, "not worth it / can't afford lawyers", and slow payment with cash-flow distress. The least-solved are IOR mismatch, finally liquidated entries, and "how much am I owed / is it worth it".

## Q3. Category detail: volume evidence, representative complaints, who is affected, and solution status

### Takeaway
Software-solvable pains (eligibility and amount estimates, ACE/ACH readiness checks, CSV pre-validation, deadline tracking, courier-IOR routing) have plenty of free *guidance* but little *tooling* aimed at sub-$500K importers. The most severe pains (finally liquidated entries, IOR mismatch, couriers' fee retention) need litigation or policy change.

### Cited Findings

#### 1. Courier refunds: waiting, opacity, who gets it
(Full courier detail is under Q4.)
- **Volume:** about 14 distinct sources. AP's coverage alone was syndicated widely (Inquirer, NBC Boston, Local10, KOB, Spectrum). Named people include a Tampa consumer waiting on UPS ([NPR via YPR](https://www.ypradio.org/2026-08-24/u-s-businesses-are-getting-tariff-refunds-will-customers-get-any-money-back)), a Texas retailer waiting on UPS and DHL ([EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/fedex-ups-and-dhl-are-filing-for-tariff-refunds-here-is-what-customers-can-expect/)), a Miami shopper billed $36 in duties and fees by FedEx ([FreightWaves via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/fedex-ups-oakley-face-lawsuits-005743545.html)), and the MavicPilots thread participants ([MavicPilots](https://mavicpilots.com/threads/refund.155555/page-2)).
- **Who:** consumers who paid duties on delivery, e-commerce sellers whose inbound parcels moved by express courier, and foreign sellers shipping DDP into the US.
- **Solution status:** FedEx's portal at ieepa-refunds.fedex.com opened Jul 10, with payouts from about Aug 10 ([KY3](https://www.ky3.com/2026/08/18/fedex-launches-new-tariff-refund-portal/); [Supply Chain Dive](https://supplychaindive.com/news/fedex-to-send-tariff-refunds-to-customers-starting-in-august/823677)). UPS has a lookup tool by tracking number ([UPS FAQ](https://www.ups.com/al/en/fragments/ieepa-faq)). These are reasonably publicized; AARP and AP ran explainers ([AARP](https://www.aarp.org/money/personal-finance/fedex-amazon-tariff-refunds/)).

#### 2. ACE portal access, login, CAPE crashes
- **Volume:** about 9 sources and at least 7 named businesses: Learning Resources, Busy Baby, Pacific International Bearing, Dame, ASM Games, and the "five or six" Main Street Alliance members ([CBS via Yahoo](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/policy/articles/tariff-refund-portal-off-bumpy-172203826.html); [Business Insider mirror](https://goodfest.westfield.com/2026/05/small-businesses-are-facing-hurdles-getting-us-tariff-refunds/); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5723862); [GovExec](https://www.govexec.com/management/2026/04/businesses-line-166b-refunds-trumps-tariffs-cbp-system-goes-live/412988/)).
- The Court of International Trade itself cited login failures, importer-of-record confusion and burdensome training events. — [Cato, May 14](https://www.cato.org/blog/us-tariff-refunds-mostly-good-developments-still-long-way-go)
- ACE setup time varied from 15 minutes for one client to two months for another, per a trade litigator. — [FoodNavigator, May 14](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/05/14/tariff-refunds-what-brands-need-to-know/)
- **Who:** small importers with dormant or never-used ACE accounts. Pacific International Bearing: "We never had the need to be active on the ACE portal." — [Business Insider mirror](https://goodfest.westfield.com/2026/05/small-businesses-are-facing-hurdles-getting-us-tariff-refunds/)
- **Solution status:** CBP added self-service password reset and a callback feature after call volumes spiked in late April ([Business Insider mirror](https://goodfest.westfield.com/2026/05/small-businesses-are-facing-hurdles-getting-us-tariff-refunds/)). Busy Baby later called the process "very simple" once she found free resources ([KSTP](https://kstp.com/kstp-news/top-news/minnesota-businesses-navigate-tariff-refund-process/)). This pain is now partly solved, but account *approval* timing still matters (see #4).

#### 3. "I'm not the importer of record": indirect importers, forwarder/broker/supplier as IOR
- **Volume:** about 10 sources and at least 4 named complainants, including a bridal shop that buys through an importer ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-5723862)), Dancing Bear Toys and unnamed gemstone and nut shops ([FT via StartMag](https://en.startmag.it/economia-2/tariff-refunds-why-small-businesses-are-angry-with-trump/)), and a yarn shop facing raised wholesale prices ([Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/money/2026/07/23/we-pay-the-tariffs-survey-chicago-illinois-small-businesses)).
- When a freight forwarder or out-of-state supplier is the IOR, "the refund legally belongs to the forwarder", and "there is no federal mechanism that forces a pass-through". — [Self Employed, May 29](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/tariff-refund-small-business-importer-of-record-2026/)
- Some customs brokers list themselves as IOR, so their client cannot get the refund until the broker files. — [Business Insider mirror](https://goodfest.westfield.com/2026/05/small-businesses-are-facing-hurdles-getting-us-tariff-refunds/)
- One guide says that for small importers who buy through wholesalers, distributors or foreign suppliers, the IOR "is almost always not you". It names marketplace sellers using 3PLs, drop-shippers and multi-tier suppliers. — [Beancount, Aug 6](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/08/06/tariff-refunds-importer-of-record-cape-portal-small-business-guide)
- Foley & Lardner calls downstream business-to-business claims over who keeps refunds the "greater risk" for importers (breach of contract, unjust enrichment, indemnification), more than consumer suits. — [Foley, Jun 18](https://www.foley.com/?p=120940)
- Non-importers may have contract pass-through claims, unjust-enrichment claims or declaratory-relief standing, and the deadlines are tight. — [Frost Law, Apr 2](https://askfrost.com/news/businesses-that-paid-tariffs-have-refund-options)
- NFIB states plainly that only businesses that *directly* paid IEEPA tariffs are eligible. — [NFIB](https://www.nfib.com/news/legal-blog/tariff-update-for-small-business-owners-and-importers/)
- **Solution status:** none statutory. The remedies are private contract claims or negotiation with the IOR. Liberty Justice Center's Project TERRA helps small businesses map entries and identify their refund path ([Self Employed](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/tariff-refund-small-business-importer-of-record-2026/)). Awareness appears low.

#### 4. Entries finally liquidated or outside the window; Phase 3 limited to plaintiffs; account approval too slow
- **Phase 1 limits:** Phase 1 covered entries that were unliquidated or liquidated within the prior 80 days. About 37% of IEEPA entries were left out, with no timeline at that point. — [Cato, May 14](https://www.cato.org/blog/us-tariff-refunds-mostly-good-developments-still-long-way-go). The 80-day window is "delays that can lead to ineligibility", per an attorney. — [Fortune, Apr 20](https://fortune.com/2026/04/20/cape-tariff-refund-portal-small-business-challenges/)
- **Phase 3 (Oct 6) eligibility:** finally liquidated entries qualify only if the filer is a plaintiff in a pending CIT case, the entry falls under a court reliquidation order, and the IOR number was submitted by Jul 30. Non-plaintiffs remain excluded while the government's appeal is pending. — [Leyton](https://leyton.com/us/insights/articles/cape-phase-3-set-to-launch-october-6-what-it-means-for-ieepa-tariff-refunds/); [C.H. Robinson, Sep 17](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-au/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2026q3/09-17-26-cbp-cape-phase-3-ieepa-refunds/)
- A secondary source says most small importers aren't covered by Phase 3. As of Oct 6, 477 Phase 3 declarations covered more than 425,000 entries. CBP's comment period on the refund framework closes Nov 4, 2026 (I have not verified this in a primary source). — [Beancount, Oct 8](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/10/08/paid-tariffs-that-no-longer-exist-ieepa-refunds-section-232-expansion-october-2026-guide)
- **Sep 9 letter:** CBP is failing to approve importer accounts within 90 days of liquidation. — [Shopappy](https://shopappy.com/trade/tariffs-customs/congress-cbp-tariff-refund-90-day-deadline)
- **Litigation option:** about 7% of refunds (about $11B) are tied up in the appeal. Lawsuits for legal challenges have a two-year statute of limitations, with the earliest deadline around February 2027. About 4,000 CIT suits had been filed. — [Bloomberg Law, Jun 29](https://news.bgov.com/in-house-counsel/small-businesses-shun-push-to-file-suits-to-get-tariff-refunds)
- **Conflict to note:** sources variously describe the window as "80 days" (CAPE Phase 1 eligibility) and "90 days" (the § 1501 reliquidation limit). — [Self Employed](https://www.selfemployed.com/news/tariff-refund-small-business-importer-of-record-2026/) vs [Shopappy](https://shopappy.com/trade/tariffs-customs/congress-cbp-tariff-refund-90-day-deadline)
- **Who:** older-entry small importers, especially those who imported early in 2025 and didn't sue. Courier informal entries are also caught here (see Q4).
- **Solution status:** a CIT lawsuit (costly; see #7) or a protest. The paths are poorly explained: the government "has not clearly explained these processes" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/20/cape-tariff-refund-portal-small-business-challenges/)).

#### 5. ACH not set up, so approved refunds are stranded
- **Mar 6:** CBP said it couldn't process about 7,700 refunds since it stopped issuing paper refunds (Feb 6). These were refunds in general, before CAPE existed. — [Retail Dive, Mar 9](https://www.retaildive.com/news/trump-tariff-refunds-cbp-cant-comply/814174/)
- **Mar 30:** 26,664 of about 330,000 eligible importers (about 8%) had signed up. They accounted for $120B, an average of more than $4.5M each. The remaining 300,000+ importers share about $46B, roughly $150K each. — [TPM, Apr 3](https://talkingpointsmemo.com/?p=1542887). **This confirms the brief's "<10% by late March".**
- **Apr 15:** about 20% of roughly 300,000 firms had enrolled (57,000). A Foley lawyer: "A smaller company may not even know about it". — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/in-house-counsel/tariff-refunds-face-delays-over-slow-importer-opt-in-us-says)
- **Stranded refunds over time:** 1,880 approved refunds were not sent to Treasury (May) ([Cato](https://www.cato.org/blog/us-tariff-refunds-mostly-good-developments-still-long-way-go)); then 4,000+ consolidated payments (June) ([EcomCrew](https://www.ecomcrew.com/tariff-refund-processing-amazon-sellers/)); then 8,384 certified refunds (July 3) ([Shopappy](https://shopappy.com/trade/tariffs-customs/cbp-tariff-refund-rollout-stumbles)); then 19,726 refunds totaling about $1.6B (Jul 31) ([World Trade Scanner](https://worldtradescanner.com/128%20bn%20IEEPA%20Tariff%20Refunded.htm)); then about $1.7B from about 10,000 importers (Aug) ([Shopappy](https://shopappy.com/trade/tariffs-customs/congress-cbp-tariff-refund-90-day-deadline)); then 20,184 refunds totaling $1.3B (mid-Sep) ([C.H. Robinson](https://www.chrobinson.com/en-au/resources/insights-and-advisories/client-advisories/2026q3/09-17-26-cbp-cape-phase-3-ieepa-refunds/)). Sources count these differently (refunds vs importers), so the series is not fully consistent.
- Bad ACH data is called "the single most common self-inflicted delay". — [TariffsTool](https://www.tariffstool.com/guides/tariff-refunds-86-billion-paid-june-surge-2026) (commercial source)
- **Who:** disproportionately small and inactive importers (inference, consistent with the TPM and Bloomberg Law data).
- **Solution status:** CBP's ACH guide and the REV-613 report flag rejected ACH refunds ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/phase-one-ieepa-tariff-refunds-are-hitting-bank-accounts-what-importers-should-do-now-and-implications-for-the-secondary-market-in-refund-rights/)). The fix is simple, but outreach is evidently not reaching about 10,000–20,000 cases.

#### 6. CAPE CSV validation errors and rejections
- **Rejection rates conflict.** About 19% of refund claims were rejected by late April ([Business Insider mirror](https://goodfest.westfield.com/2026/05/small-businesses-are-facing-hurdles-getting-us-tariff-refunds/); [Cato](https://www.cato.org/blog/us-tariff-refunds-mostly-good-developments-still-long-way-go)). FreightFigures reports a 15.5% *full pass* rate on entries in week 1 ([FreightFigures](https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/cape-declaration-rejected-top-10-validation-failures-2026)). A trade litigator cited roughly 40% rejection ([FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/05/14/tariff-refunds-what-brands-need-to-know/)). The 4.36M entries that failed validation by July 3 were mostly correctable data problems ([Shopappy](https://shopappy.com/trade/tariffs-customs/cbp-tariff-refund-rollout-stumbles)).
- **Typical causes:** submitter identity or filer-code mismatch; invalid 11-character entry numbers; duplicate entries; entry types 08/09/23/47; no IEEPA Chapter 99 line ([FreightFigures](https://www.freightfigures.com/articles/cape-declaration-rejected-top-10-validation-failures-2026)). A declaration cannot be amended once accepted, and each entry can appear on only one declaration ([eCommerce Fastlane](https://ecommercefastlane.com/tariff-refund-shopify-importers-cape-portal/)). Claims have also contained wrong amounts and misclassified goods; one example was a handbag misclassified as a turboprop part ([Business Insider mirror](https://goodfest.westfield.com/2026/05/small-businesses-are-facing-hurdles-getting-us-tariff-refunds/)).
- **Solution status:** brokers, CBP error documentation, and many vendor and blog guides. This is well served by guides and partly by tools (vendor detail is covered elsewhere).

#### 7. "Not worth it / can't afford lawyers / no trade counsel"

Some small firms closed, or may decide a refund isn't worth the effort and take a tax loss instead ([TPM](https://talkingpointsmemo.com/?p=1542887)). Small businesses tend to wait and see which strategy yields the most ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/04/20/cape-tariff-refund-portal-small-business-challenges/)).

- **Solution status:** pro bono help (Liberty Justice Center for Princess Awesome and Burlap & Barrel) and contingency lawyers (Sarah Wells recouped her full $20K) ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bgov.com/in-house-counsel/small-businesses-shun-push-to-file-suits-to-get-tariff-refunds); [FoodNavigator](https://www.foodnavigator.com/Article/2026/05/14/tariff-refunds-what-brands-need-to-know/)). Both are scarce and poorly known.

## Q4. Courier and e-commerce angle (UPS, FedEx, DHL, USPS; class actions; de minimis; how many small sellers)

### Takeaway
UPS, FedEx and DHL all file CAPE claims for entries where *they* were the importer of record and pass refunds to whoever paid the duty. None refund their brokerage or admin fees. UPS pays out 60–90 days after it receives funds and nets refunds against open invoices first. DHL can take up to about 180 days in total. FedEx prioritizes customers who opt in to sharing data. The big unaddressed hole: Phase 1 only covered courier *small-parcel* duties paid from about Jan 30, 2026. Most 2025 parcel entries appear to be finally liquidated, and UPS says it has not sued at the CIT for its IOR entries, which Phase 3 requires. Many 2025 courier-paid duties may therefore have no current path (inference). USPS has no announced policy. More than 80 class actions are pending; none certified.

### Inferences
- **(Inference, significant) Many 2025 parcel duties have no path today.** DHL and UPS both limit Phase 1 small-parcel coverage to duties paid from about Jan 30, 2026. The likely reason is that informal and low-value entries liquidate quickly and most 2025 ones were finally liquidated by April. Phase 3 requires a CIT plaintiff, and UPS says it has not sued for its IOR entries. So many 2025 UPS-handled parcel duties (and possibly DHL's) have no current refund path. UPS paid about $5B but filed about $0.5B in Phase 1, roughly 10%, which fits this gap. FedEx did sue, so FedEx-IOR finally liquidated entries may be eligible for Phase 3 if a court order covers them. This needs verification by the CBP-mechanics researcher, but it is the single largest unexplained hole for e-commerce sellers and consumers.
- (Inference) For small sellers whose inbound shipments went by courier, the IOR is usually the courier for low-value parcels and often the seller for formal entries. RefundRadar could route each shipment to "courier portal (no action)", "you are IOR, so file CAPE", or "no path; litigation or wait".
- (Inference) The prospects of recovering courier fees through litigation are uncertain (arbitration clauses, FAAA preemption). Software can at least itemize the fees that were not refunded as evidence for a class-action claim.

## Q5. Top 5 unmet pains (severity × volume × lack of good solution), and what software can fix

### Takeaway
The top unmet pains are: (1) entries aging out or locked out by liquidation, deadlines and the Phase 3 plaintiff rule; (2) "I'm not the IOR" for indirect importers and courier or forwarder IOR cases; (3) "is this worth it and how much am I owed" paralysis; (4) ACE/ACH readiness and stranded approved refunds; (5) opaque courier pass-through, including the uncovered 2025 parcel duties and the fees nobody refunds. Software can substantially fix #3 and #4, partly fix #1 and #5 (triage and alerts), and only route around #2. The core fixes for #1, #2 and #5 need courts or policy.

### Inferences
Scoring is my judgment on a 1–5 scale. Priority = Severity × Volume × Unsolved.

| Rank | Pain | Severity | Volume | Unsolved | Priority | What software can do | Needs legal or policy change? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Entries aging out or locked out** (finally liquidated, 80/90-day window, Phase 3 plaintiff-only, slow ACE approval) | 5 (permanent loss) | 4 | 5 | 100 | Compute each entry's liquidation date and days left; flag "at risk this week"; classify each entry as CAPE Phase 1/2, needs protest, needs CIT suit, or wait for the non-plaintiff ruling; export a protest-ready list and a CIT-referral packet; alert on CBP phase changes | Yes. Non-plaintiff final-liquidation refunds depend on the appeal or a CBP policy change; tolling the 90-day clock needs CBP action (requested in the Sep 9 letter) |
| 2 | **"I'm not the IOR"** (supplier, forwarder, broker or courier is IOR) | 4 | 5 (most small retailers) | 5 | 100 | Detect IOR from 7501 box 26 or carrier data; tell the user "the refund belongs to X"; generate a demand letter citing contract pass-through clauses; route courier-IOR shipments to FedEx/UPS/DHL portals | Mostly yes. No statutory pass-through; remedies are contract or unjust-enrichment claims |
| 3 | **"How much am I owed, and is it worth it?" paralysis** (no trade counsel, fear of lawyer cost, predatory 30% fees) | 3 | 5 | 4 | 60 | Free estimate of IEEPA duty plus interest per entry from ACE reports or 7501s; a "DIY vs broker vs lawyer" calculator against published fees (for example Clearit's $200 + 4%); a fair-fee benchmark that exposes 30% contingency offers | No. Software can fix this largely |
| 4 | **ACE/ACH readiness and stranded approved refunds** | 3 (money approved but stuck) | 3 (about 10K–20K refunds) | 3 (fix exists but isn't reaching people) | 27 | Readiness checklist (active ACE account owner, ACH refund enrollment, 5106 up to date, dormant-user cleanup); parse REV-613 and ES-022 reports to spot stranded or rejected refunds; reminders | No. Software and outreach can fix it |
| 5 | **Courier pass-through opacity** (which shipments, when, uncovered 2025 parcels, fees not refunded) | 2–3 per person (small amounts, many people) | 4 | 3 | 24–36 | Aggregate tracking numbers across FedEx/UPS/DHL; tell the user which lookup tool to use and whether the shipment falls in a covered Phase 1 window or the gap; itemize unrefunded fees for class-action claim records; warn about scam portals | Partly. Fees and the 2025 parcel gap need litigation (courier CIT suits or class actions) |

Runners-up: CAPE CSV pre-validation (high early volume, but already served by brokers and vendor tools); scams and claim buyers (software can help with a trust and benchmark layer); small firms' own pass-through obligations (an allocation calculator could help).

- (Inference) For a hackathon MVP due Oct 30, the strongest wedge is to combine #3 (estimate) with #1 (per-entry deadline and pathway triage) and #4 (readiness check). All three are data problems a tool can solve from an uploaded ACE ES-003-style entry report or 7501 PDFs. Courier routing (#5) is a cheap add-on for e-commerce users.
- (Inference) Be honest in-product. For finally liquidated entries owned by non-plaintiffs, and for indirect importers, the right answer is "no CAPE path; here are your options and deadlines", not a promise of a refund.

### Gaps
- Severity, volume and unsolved scores are judgment calls; no dataset weights them.
- I could not verify the share of small importers whose entries are finally liquidated, or the count of non-plaintiff small importers locked out of Phase 3.
- Whether the government's appeal on non-plaintiff final-liquidation refunds has been decided as of Oct 25, 2026 was not established in this research. The CBP-mechanics researcher should confirm.
